Abstract

We present Video2SwimFish, an automated pipeline and benchmark for building controllable fish assets from real-fish videos for underwater embodied AI. Given synchronized multi-view videos of an individual fish, the pipeline generates a metrically scaled deformable mesh from a VLM-selected canonical frame, builds internal articulation adapted to that individual’s morphology through a VLM actor–critic loop, and extracts a Biological Locomotion Manifold (BLM). The BLM provides a low-dimensional action space bounded by real-fish motion. Compared with learning directly in joint space, it lets the individual swimming policy of each reconstructed fish converge faster and reach a higher task success rate. We release two paired datasets: synchronized top- and front-view recordings of 120 individual fish across 6 species, and the controllable assets and individual swimming policies derived from them. Because every asset is tied to the animal it came from, the dataset supports a benchmark that evaluates locomotion learning not only on task success but on fidelity to that individual’s locomotion behavior. We evaluate on three tasks: 3D trajectory following, target reaching, and capture of the assets by a simulated BlueROV underwater robot, together with a human study in which fish experts judge the biological realism of the simulated fish, complementing automated evaluation metrics. We find that task success and locomotion fidelity do not necessarily improve together, and we identify faithful reproduction of individual animal locomotion as an open challenge for the community.

Dataset Display

Synchronized top- and front-view recordings of 120 individual fish across six species, with 20 individuals per species. Each fish is recorded alone for two and a half minutes at 32 fps and 2736 × 1824 resolution.

Pipeline Overview

Reconstruction and Articulation

3D Mesh Generation

Qwen3-VL selects a canonical frame, and Meshy generates a mesh scaled to the measured body length. Across all 120 fish, mean affine-aligned silhouette IoU is 0.91 in the input side view and 0.75 in the unseen top view.

VLM-based Articulation

An actor VLM proposes bone templates, positions, and sizes; a critic VLM scores the articulation and returns feedback. The final bone chain is coupled to the deformable fish body in the simulator.

Biological Locomotion Manifold

Synchronized midlines are fused into a 3D body midline and mapped into lateral and vertical joint angles by inverse kinematics. Eight PCA modes capture 99.6% of the joint-angle variance on average and provide the individual fish's action space.

Benchmark

Locomotion learning is evaluated on task success and biological fidelity. The two swimming tasks use twelve fish, two per species, and three random seeds. The ROV case study uses six fish, one per species. Twelve expert raters assess 25 blinded Task 1 clips.

Results

Task 1: 3D Trajectory Following

BLM + RL completes 0.70 of held-out paths and covers 0.85 of real-fish bending motions. Its median training run reaches a completion rate of 0.5 in 20 epochs, compared with 32 for BCO + RL and 70 for Joint RL.

Task 2: Target Reaching

BLM + RL reaches 9.0 of 10 targets per episode, with a timeout rate of 0.10 and motion coverage of 0.96, averaged over twelve fish.

Case Study: BlueROV Capture

All six capture policies succeed in at least 98% of evaluation episodes, with 381 successful captures out of 384 episodes in total. Training converges within 145–217 epochs.

Human Study: Perceived Biological Fidelity

Twelve fish experts rate 25 blinded clips from three fish and five held-out trajectories. BLM + RL receives the highest mean plausibility (4.48/5), path match (3.65/5), and overall similarity (4.07/5); CPG + RL receives the highest speed match (4.92/5). The mean appearance score across all clips is 4.9/5.

Limitations

The simulations use an approximate quasi-steady hydrodynamic model rather than full fluid–structure interaction. Sim-to-real transfer has not been tested. Due to compute limits, the benchmark evaluates twelve of the 120 fish, with six in the capture case study. BLM + RL's advantages over a well-tuned CPG are small compared with variation across individual fish.
